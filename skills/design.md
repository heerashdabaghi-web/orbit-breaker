# Design Skill

Use this guide when defining or improving the game's rules, feel, interface, and presentation.

## Design Process

1. State the game in one sentence: action, objective, and pressure.
2. Identify the core loop: what the player repeatedly observes, decides, and does.
3. Prototype that loop with simple shapes before adding decorative detail.
4. Tune the opening 30 seconds so the player can learn safely.
5. Increase difficulty through one or two readable variables at a time.
6. Add feedback that makes cause and effect obvious.

## Interaction Principles

- Prefer a small set of precise controls over many weak actions.
- Make input response immediate; use animation to communicate motion, not delay control.
- Give hazards a recognizable silhouette and enough warning to react.
- Use screen shake, flashes, particles, and sound sparingly so important moments stand out.
- Keep HUD information near the action without covering it.
- Make primary buttons visually dominant and large enough for touch.

## Difficulty Tuning

- Begin below the expected player's maximum ability.
- Increase challenge smoothly using speed, density, timing, patterns, or decision complexity.
- Avoid unavoidable damage and unpredictable rule changes.
- Provide brief recovery windows after major mistakes or transitions.
- Track score in a way that rewards the intended play style.

## Accessibility Checks

- Maintain readable contrast and font sizes.
- Pair color signals with shape, text, motion, or sound.
- Avoid rapid flashing and excessive camera motion.
- Provide reduced-motion behavior when effects are intense.
- Ensure keyboard focus is visible on menus and buttons.

## Design Review Questions

- Can a new player explain the objective after one attempt?
- Is every failure traceable to a visible cause?
- Does the game become more interesting rather than merely faster?
- Is the most important object always easy to locate?
- Does restarting feel instant?
